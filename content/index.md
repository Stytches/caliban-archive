## Goals: [[Caliban_Philosophy|The Pedagogy of Dust]]

Caliban operates as a "Good Nightmare," driven by a twisted Seelie code to strengthen the world through fear. He believes Kindred society has grown soft and careless, so he administers "kindly warnings"—calculated traumas and exposed secrets designed to force vigilance. His goal is not to destroy, but to teach; he breaks bones so they knit back stronger, ensuring his allies are hardened enough to survive the coming Winter.

## Specialties Explained

### Linguistics: Latin (Occult Focus)

**What it means:**  
Caliban is a classically trained scholar (Language: **Latin**), but his education didn't stop at grammar. He specializes in texts written to *conceal* rather than communicate—alchemical manuscripts, hermetic warnings, and the coded journals of paranoid elders. He reads the "silence" between the words.

### Combat Style: The Geometry of Decay

**Weapons:** Rapier (Sword Cane) & Main-Gauche (Parrying Dagger).

**Philosophy:**  
Caliban does not "brawl"; he corrects errors in his opponent's biology. He uses a bastardized form of **Florentine fencing** taught by his Sire. The rapier (often disguised as a gentleman's cane) creates the boundary ("Geometry"), keeping the chaotic world at bay. The main-gauche (hidden in his coat) is the "Scalpel" used for the kill.

**Mechanically in V20:**  

- He fights with **Dexterity (4) + Melee (3)**.
- **Two-Weapon Fighting:** Lacking the Ambidexterity merit, he often **splits his dice pool** to fight defensively (Parry + Strike). When striking with his off-hand (main-gauche), he suffers a **+2 difficulty penalty**. However, he can choose to commit to a **single action**—either a focused attack with the rapier or a dedicated **Full Defense**—to utilize his full dice pool (7 dice) without penalty. This allows him to switch between a "whittling" defensive style and sudden, precise lethality.
- **The Sword Cane:** Allows him to carry a lethal weapon into Elysium or high-society functions without breaking etiquette. It reinforces his "Old Money" facade.

### Stealth – Specialty: Moving Through Shadows

This specialty is not a formal rule term from the book, but a thematically appropriate, and emphasizes how Caliban uses darkness and shadow as his natural medium. It covers slipping from patch to patch of darkness, hugging walls, vanishing behind pillars, and using Obfuscate as a natural extension of his physical stealth.

**Mechanically in V20:**  

- As with all specialties, when **Dexterity + Stealth** is rolled for an action where "moving through shadows" clearly applies (nighttime, dim corridors, alleys, unlit rooms, etc.), **any 10s on the roll count as 2 successes instead of 1**.  
- It most often applies when:
  - Sneaking through darkened streets, abandoned buildings, sewers, tunnels, or basements.  
  - Stalking prey in low light, especially when you can move from one shadow to another.  
  - Performing **ambushes** using Obfuscate 2 (Unseen Presence) from cover. The standard V20 ambush rule is **Dexterity + Stealth vs. Perception + Alertness**; if you win, you get an undefended attack and add extra successes to your attack pool.  

**In play:** When describing Caliban’s movement as melting into alcoves, doorframes, or broken patches of light and shade, this signals that he is intending to engage his **Moving Through Shadows** specialty. This reinforces the [[Sluagh]] theme: he doesn’t just hide, he *belongs* to dark corners.

## Nature and Demeanor Embodied

### Nature: Pedagogue

At his core, Caliban is driven to **teach**, even when his methods are cruel, indirect, or terrifying. Pedagogue means:

- He regains Willpower when someone **genuinely learns** from him—when a coterie member takes his warning to heart, when a mortal faces a fear he orchestrated and comes out stronger, when even an enemy walks away changed by the horror he revealed.  
- He frames his entire existence as an ongoing curriculum: every conversation is a lesson, every secret is a carefully chosen reading assignment, every scar is a grade written into the flesh.  
- He is patient but uncompromising. If a student refuses to learn gently, he escalates the lesson until reality itself forces the point. In his mind, this is mercy: a painful truth is better than a comforting lie that gets you killed.
- **The Glint:** There is a dry, dusty humor in his lessons. He appreciates the irony of a monster teaching humanity to humans. When a plan comes together perfectly, or a student finally "gets it" in a moment of sheer panic, he might offer a rare, thin smile—not of cruelty, but of satisfaction at a well-told joke.

**In character:** when he asks a price for a secret, he is not being petty—he is ensuring the recipient is *invested* enough to actually grow from what he is about to reveal. And perhaps, he is also enjoying the look on their face when they realize the cost.

### Demeanor: Traditionalist

On the surface, Caliban appears as a **clinging ghost of old etiquette**. Traditionalist means:

- He presents himself as someone who believes deeply in rules, forms, and proper procedure: asking permission to enter, bowing to the Prince, using titles, respecting domains and thresholds.  
- Others see him as conservative, rigid, or anachronistic. His threadbare but fine-cut clothing, his whispered honorifics, and his rituals of address all signal that he lives in a different, older social logic.  
- He often couches his advice and warnings in the language of protocol: "It is improper to invite the rot without first preparing the house," or "It would be… discourteous to let the silence fester without addressing it, my Prince."

**In character:** the Traditionalist mask is both a shield and a teaching tool. It makes him easy to underestimate as "just a stuffy relic," which gives him room to observe. It also gives him a framework to **impose** structure on others: by insisting on certain forms of address, formal bargains, and the paying of prices, he forces people to slow down and *think*—which is exactly what a Pedagogue wants. Plus, watching a Brujah try to bow correctly is endlessly entertaining.

## Origin: The Hollowed Kinain

In life, Caliban was "Kinain"—a human carrying dormant fae blood, specifically of [[Sluagh]] heritage. He was always weird, quiet, and able to see things others couldn't, waiting for a "Chrysalis" (awakening) that never came.

The Embrace destroyed his chance to become a changeling. The Curse of Caine killed the fae spark within him, leaving a gaping, spiritual wound. His Malkavian madness rushed in to fill that void, calcifying into a twisted parody of what he *should* have been.

He is a "Rotten [[Sluagh]]." He has all the instincts, the etiquette, and the affinity for shadows, but it is cold, dead, and static. Real changelings find him repulsive—like looking at a taxidermied relative. His fractured mind still receives "echoes" from the Dreaming, but they are distorted, nightmarish, and distant.

He possesses a deep, melancholy longing for the "Dreaming" that he can never touch. He collects fae trinkets not out of desire for magic, but out of grief.

## Flaws and Their Presentation

Caliban’s flaws are not random weaknesses; they are facets of his [[Sluagh]]-like condition. Each one should be played as part of the same fae etiquette curse, reinforcing the sense that he is the ghost of a long-dead gentleman bound by old rules.

### [[Speech Impediment]] (Mental, 1 pt)

**Mechanics:** The difficulties of all die rolls involving verbal communication are increased by two.

**Presentation:** Caliban is physically and psychologically incapable of speaking above a whisper. His voice is always low, dry, and close, forcing others to lean in or quiet down if they want to hear him. When he is forced to raise his voice (gunfire, chaos, arguments), the words catch and break in his throat. He may cough, stammer, or simply go silent, eyes wide with almost childlike panic at his own breach of decorum. This reinforces the [[Sluagh]] “Law of Whispers” and makes every spoken word feel intentional, intimate, and just a little invasive.

### [[Eerie Presence]] (Supernatural, 2 pts)

**Mechanics:** Difficulties of all rolls relating to social interaction with mortals are increased by two.

**Presentation:** Caliban is unsettling even when he does nothing. His gaunt frame, moth-eaten but once-elegant clothes, and distant gaze make rooms feel colder and quieter when he enters. Lights seem slightly dimmer around him; people find themselves lowering their voices without knowing why. This should be played as an almost environmental effect: conversations trail off when he steps into a room, humans feel watched even from across Elysium, and even other Kindred instinctively avoid turning their backs on him. It is not overt menace, but the social equivalent of sitting too close to a crypt door.

### [[Touch of Frost]] (Supernatural, 1 pt)

**Mechanics:** Plants wither as he approaches and die at his touch. His touch leeches heat from living beings, as though he were made of ice.

**Presentation:** Prolonged contact with Caliban chills the skin and fogs the breath. Handshakes linger as an unpleasant cold ache in the bones; a touch to the shoulder leaves a faint, numb tingling that takes minutes to fade. When he brushes by someone, they may shiver and pull their coat tighter, even if the ambient temperature is normal. This emphasizes his "long-dead gentleman" quality, as though a draft from the grave follows him. He uses this sparingly, often as a wordless punctuation to a lesson: a brief, gloved hand on the wrist as he whispers a hard truth.

### [[Shy]] (Mental, 1 pt)

**Mechanics:** Difficulties for all rolls involving social interaction with strangers are increased by two. If he becomes the center of attention in a large group, difficulties are increased by three.

**Presentation:** Caliban does not initiate social contact unless ritual demands it. In crowds, he clings to walls, doorframes, and corners, observing until someone *invites* him in—literally or figuratively. When addressed unexpectedly, he averts his eyes for a heartbeat, smoothing his lapel or cuff before responding. This shyness is not cute or bumbling; it is brittle, haunted. The [[Sluagh]] etiquette in his head insists that he is an intruder in any social space until properly welcomed, and he will endure significant inconvenience rather than impose.

### [[Nightmares]] (Mental, 1 pt)

**Mechanics:** Upon awakening, he must make a Willpower roll (difficulty 7) or lose a die on all actions for that night. A botched Willpower roll indicates that, even when awake, he still believes that he is locked in a nightmare.

**Presentation:** By day, his slumber is not restful but crowded with whispering figures, dripping cellars, and endless, echoing courtrooms where invisible judges deliberate over the souls of those he has "taught." These nightmares can be used by the Storyteller to feed him cryptic warnings, twisted allegories, or emotional echoes of upcoming threats. Upon waking, he often performs a brief, silent ritual—straightening his clothes, bowing to an empty corner—before interacting with anyone, as if acknowledging unseen observers from his dreams. The persistent sleep-terrors contribute to his gauntness and the sense that he is fraying at the edges.

### [[Tic-Twitch|Tic/Twitch]] (Physical, 1 pt)

**Mechanics:** It costs one Willpower to refrain from engaging in the tic.

**Presentation:** Caliban has a small, involuntary motion that becomes more pronounced under stress—a faint tremor in his fingers when he folds his hands, a recurring adjustment of his collar, a tiny tilt of the head as if listening to something just behind his ear. When his rituals are strained or broken, the tic escalates: fingers drum silently in complex, repetitive patterns; his head cocks at increasingly sharp angles, eyes staring at nothing. This tic should be a visual metronome for the table: the more frequent or sharp it gets, the closer he is to losing his delicate hold on his Obsessive Ritualism.

## Derangement: Obsessive Ritualism ([[Sluagh]] Etiquette)

His madness is not loud or chaotic; it is a suffocating lattice of rules, taboos, and courtesies that he *cannot* break without severe psychological distress. He moves through the World of Darkness as if it is an ancient, haunted court, governed by old laws that only he remembers. These rituals are how he keeps the howling, whispering chaos in his mind ordered and contained.

Mechanically, this functions as an obsessive-compulsive derangement: when he is prevented from following his rituals, or forced to violate them, he suffers increasing anxiety, distraction, and eventually full-blown panic or fugue. The Storyteller may impose penalties to dice pools, temporary loss of Willpower, or force him into a withdrawal/"reset" scene until he can reassert his etiquette.

Psychologically, he frames these rituals not as superstition but as *necessary psychic discipline*. He genuinely believes that if he and others observe these courtesies, minds will be strengthened, not broken, by the horror and truth he reveals.

**Clinical Perspective:**
While Kindred psychology is distinct from mortal pathology, a modern clinician might classify this behavior as **Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)** with **Schizotypal features**.

- **OCD Parallels:** The intrusive thoughts of "chaos" are neutralized only by rigid, specific behaviors (rituals). Preventing these rituals causes severe distress, anxiety, and panic.
- **Schizotypal Parallels:** The belief that these rituals literally hold back reality or that misaddressing someone "lies to the fabric of reality" aligns with magical thinking. The social anxiety and odd speech patterns further support this classification.

### Core Tenets of His Ritualism

1. **Permission to Enter**  
   He may not cross a threshold—doorways, gates, even the invisible boundary of a claimed space—without first asking permission. This can be literal ("May I enter?") or symbolic (a bow toward a doorway, a whispered request to the room, a glance to the presumed "host"). If no one answers, he will wait several heartbeats, repeat the request more softly, and only then step through, stiff and wary, like someone knowingly violating sacred ground.

2. **The Law of Whispers**  
   He never raises his voice above a soft, carrying whisper. To speak loudly is to wound the world. When forced to address a crowd, he lets silence do most of the work: people must lean in, quiet themselves, come closer. This is not only a quirk; it is a *test*. Those who make the effort to listen prove themselves worthy of the secret they seek.

3. **The Price of Secrets**  
   No secret is ever free. Payment can be blood, boons, information, humiliation, or a trial he sets. He *must* exact a price, both for his own equilibrium and for the supplicant's growth. He sincerely believes that the cost itself is part of the medicine; suffering and struggle temper the psyche.

4. **The Ritual of Address**  
   He does not speak to anyone without first silently or softly naming them in his mind according to their role: "Prince," "Supplicant," "Offender," "Child," "Guest." This need not be spoken aloud every time, but he often lets an honorific slip: "My Prince," "dear supplicant," "honored guest." Misaddressing someone feels like lying to the fabric of reality.

5. **The Sanctity of Silence**  
   Interrupting someone mid-confession, mid-conflict, or mid-breakdown is abhorrent to him. Once a psyche begins to crack open in his presence, he feels a compulsion to *see it through*. If others try to drag the person away or shut them up, he becomes tense, brittle, and may intervene—verbally or even physically—to "let the wound drain."

6. **The Trial of the Mirror**  
   When someone asks for a particularly dangerous or transformative secret, he must first make them confront themselves. He asks invasive questions, insists they recount a shame, or forces them to watch someone else suffer in a way that reflects their own flaws. Only then does he share what they came for.

### Internal Consequences of Breaking Ritual

When he is forced to break one of his rules—entering without permission, shouting, giving a secret freely—the following internal reactions guide his behavior:

1. **Immediate Shock:** He freezes for a heartbeat, eyes unfocused, as if listening to a chorus of disapproving whispers only he can hear.
2. **Creeping Contamination:** He begins to feel physically unclean or "out of alignment." He may straighten his clothes, dust his sleeves, or smooth his hair compulsively.
3. **Ritual Repair:** As soon as possible, he seeks to *repair* the breach: asking retroactive permission, whispering apologies to the room, or demanding some price to "balance" the free secret he gave away.
4. **Escalating Discomfort:** If prevented from repairing, he grows distracted and brittle. The Storyteller can apply penalties as his mind spirals. His voice may falter; his hands tremble.
5. **Collapse or Retreat:** In extreme cases, he withdraws from the scene entirely to perform a more elaborate purification—silent hours in the dark, self-imposed hunger, or a dangerous vision-quest into his own derangement.

### Operational Loopholes (Stealth & Breaking Entry)

Since Caliban is a stealth specialist, his inability to enter without permission is a significant tactical hurdle. He utilizes specific "loopholes" in his own madness to function:

1. **The Vanguard Proxy:** The most efficient method. A coterie member breaks in first, "claims" the room (even if they have only been there for a second), and whispers, "Caliban, enter." To Caliban, this transfers authority; the ally is now the "host" of the space, and their invitation is valid.
2. **The "Yielding" Mechanism:** If Caliban uses Larceny to pick a lock or crack a safe, he views the mechanism's defeat as a form of consent. The lock wrestled with him and lost; by opening, it has "bowed" and granted him passage. This allows for solo breaking-and-entering, provided *he* is the one who defeats the barrier.
3. **The Silence of Abandonment:** As noted in the tenets, if he asks and no one answers, he *can* enter after a delay. He interprets the silence of an empty room as "passive permission." This is too slow for combat or chases but sufficient for careful infiltration.
4. **The Hostile Breach (Willpower Expenditure):** When stealth is paramount and asking permission is impossible (e.g., entering a room with guards), Caliban may spend **1 Willpower point** to force himself across the threshold. He frames this internally as a "hostile takeover"—he is not a guest, but an invader. This bypasses the need to speak, but the psychological strain counts as a minor breach of ritual (triggering *Creeping Contamination* once the immediate danger passes).
