# The Tithe of Breath: Caliban's Feeding Habits

**Philosophy:**
To Caliban, the Kiss is not an act of violence, but a transaction. He calls it "The Tithe." The world is a noisy, chaotic place, and he is merely collecting the silence that is owed to the dark. He does not hunt like a wolf; he collects like a taxman.

He views blood not just as fuel, but as **memory** and **momentum**. When he drinks, he is stopping a clock for a few moments, stealing time from someone else to add to his own frozen existence.

---

## Preferred Vessels

### 1. The Forgotten (The Gentle Tithe)

* **Who:** The elderly living alone, night-shift security guards, archivists, the lonely.
* **Why:** They are already half-ghosts. They understand silence. Their blood tastes like dust, old paper, and cobwebs—a flavor Caliban finds comforting.
* **Method:** He approaches them with reverence. He often sits with them for a moment (unseen or unnoticed) before feeding. He is always careful, never taking enough to harm. He views this as sharing a burden.
* **The Glint:** He operates on the logic of a **magpie**. He leaves a "receipt" for what he takes—a fixed latch on their window, a book they had lost, or a silver coin. It is a trade: breath for a trinket. He considers himself a polite guest, even if they didn't know he was there.

### 2. The Careless (The Punitive Tithe)

* **Who:** The loud, the arrogant, the braggarts, the ones who walk down dark alleys screaming at their phones, each other, the world.
* **Why:** They are wasting their breath. They treat their vitality as something cheap. It is a tax on stupidity.
* **Method:** This is a lesson. He strikes from the shadows, terrifying them first. He takes more from them—enough to leave them weak and shaken for days. He whispers a warning into their ear before he leaves: *"Shhh. You are bruising the silence. It does not like to be touched so roughly."*

### 3. The Guilty (The Confessional Tithe)

* **Who:** Those hiding a secret that is eating them alive.
* **Why:** Caliban imagines he can smell the rot of a bad conscience. *Given enough time, he may well accomplish this feat for real one day*
* **Method:** He acts as a sin-eater. He drinks the blood that is tainted by their guilt. It is a heavy, bitter meal, but it sustains his own melancholy. He enjoys the irony of draining the life from someone who was already destroying themselves from the inside.

---

## The Taboo (The Unpalatable)

He **refuses** to feed on children or the truly innocent (those who have not yet accumulated "dust"). Their blood is too bright, too loud, and too chaotic. It burns him like sunlight. It is like trying to drink static electricity.

He also avoids the highly intoxicated. While his Sluagh instincts draw him to decay, he distinguishes between the **dry rot** of age (which he savors) and the **wet rot** of poison. To him, a drunkard is not decaying; they are merely fermenting—a sloppy, noisy process he finds undignified.
